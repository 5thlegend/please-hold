Please Hold

One sentence in. The whole customer-service errand out.

The errand you keep putting off: the refund, the cancellation, the bill that jumped $30. Type it once. Gemma, running on your own computer, writes the phone script, the email and the chat message. Then a receipts check removes anything you never said.

What you get

Phone script with the opener, the ask, three lines for when they say no, and the close. Plus an email, a chat message, hand-written rights pointers with official sources, hold mode with your own hold music, and an after-call confirmation email and reminder built from your notes.

The rule

The model writes words, never facts. Every fact has to quote you. Every phone number, URL, amount, date, deadline and law in the output is checked against what you typed. Invented contact details are removed; anything else you didn't say gets highlighted.

02

Build log

Oct 20–23, 2026

In order, including the parts that went wrong.

STEP 1
brief

Week 3 is open, five days left

Theme: build something with open-weight AI that rewards laziness. We pitched three ideas. The pick was an offline bedtime companion that slows down with you.

STEP 2
research

Go read what people actually complain about

Four research tracks: life admin, household and food, sleep, developer chores. The loudest pain wasn't sleep or inboxes. It was dealing with companies: phone trees, hold time, subscriptions that won't cancel. Calm sits at 1.4/5 on Trustpilot, mostly billing-trap reviews.

STEP 3
pivot

Our first idea was taken four times

A check of the challenge tag found Sloth Companion, SnoozeScape, Compline and SlumberSafe: all bedtime companions. Fridge apps and file sorters were crowded too. Nobody had built for the customer's side of the call. New pick: Please Hold.

STEP 4
build

The plan: Gemma 4 in the browser

Gemma 4 E2B in the tab through WebLLM, your own Ollama as an option, and an opt-in Gemma 4 26B on Cloudflare for phones. Same open family, you pick where it runs.

STEP 5
build

The receipts check

Plain JavaScript, no model. It finds every checkable claim in Gemma's output and compares it to your sentence. Unit tests passed on the first run, which never happens.

STEP 6
eval

24 errands through the cloud model

The cloud model came back clean on the first try: no invented contact details, a few flags where Gemma did its own math ("$110" from "$120, supposed to be $10"). The math is right, but you never said it, so you get asked to confirm.

STEP 7
bug

CacheStorage error, then CORS, then curl says it's fine

The on-device model wouldn't download from the live site. Replaying Chrome's exact request with curl, one header at a time: Hugging Face returns 404 with no CORS headers whenever the Referer is a *.workers.dev page.

STEP 8
fix

One meta tag

<meta name="referrer" content="no-referrer"> plus a Referrer-Policy header. All model files returned 200 on the next try.

STEP 9
bug

The browser said no, three ways

Gemma 4 E2B on WebLLM 0.2.86 lost the GPU on its first token, and so did every model on that version. Transformers.js downloaded all 3.3 GB, then the tab crashed. Gemma 3 1B ran, but printed whitespace under a JSON grammar, and without one it invented a "$10.99 on Oct 28" charge. The user had typed $49.99 and Oct 3.

STEP 10
pivot

Local means Ollama

Gemma 3 4B in Ollama runs on the same laptop GPU with native CUDA. The site finds it automatically, the cloud stays opt-in, and the in-browser model lives behind ?lab=1 with an honest write-up.

STEP 11
fix

Customer's voice, not the company's

The first local kit opened with "Thank you for contacting Lumen Fitness, I'm here to assist you today." Gemma had written the agent's lines. One prompt rule later: everything in the customer's voice, said to the company.

STEP 12
eval

Final numbers, both backends

48 runs, 324 checkable claims. 316 matched the user's words, 8 flagged (all Gemma's own arithmetic), 0 invented contact details.
